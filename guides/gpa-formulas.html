One transcript.
Four different GPA views.

“What is your GPA?” is often the wrong first question for a Canadian medical-school list. The useful question is: what GPA will this school construct from this transcript?

Why cGPA alone misleads

Schools do not all reward the same academic pattern. A rising trend can matter at a best-year school and barely move a cumulative formula. A full course load can determine whether a year qualifies. A percentage-based school can apply an adjustment that does not map neatly to an OMSAS number.

That means the same applicant can present four materially different academic profiles without changing a single grade.

Four official examples

McMaster: cumulative means cumulative

McMaster states that it uses a cumulative average across all undergraduate courses, treats years equally, and does not eliminate a weak year. Its published pre-interview formula then combines GPA, CARS, and CASPer.

Decision implication: a strong late year helps only through its effect on the full average; it does not replace an earlier year.

Official source: McMaster, Before You Apply

Western: two qualifying years, not simply your top two numbers

Western requires the minimum GPA in each of two qualifying undergraduate years. Its official requirements also define course-load, course-level, and pass/fail conditions for a year to qualify.

Decision implication: sorting yearly averages and taking the top two is not enough. First verify that both years satisfy the structural rules.

Official source: Western admission requirements

U of T: cumulative floor, current-year timing

U of T publishes a cumulative OMSAS-scale minimum for undergraduate applicants and explains that the current year is not part of the application-period calculation because those grades are unavailable.

Decision implication: a planned final-year recovery may not change the number used during the current application cycle.

Official source: U of T academic requirements

UBC: percentage averages and an adjusted calculation

UBC evaluates an overall academic average in percentage terms and describes an adjusted average that may remove up to 30 credits from the lowest academic year when its conditions are met. It also publishes different full-file-review thresholds for BC and out-of-province applicants.

Decision implication: an OMSAS cGPA is not a drop-in substitute. Course conversion, adjustment eligibility, and applicant pool all need separate checks.

Official source: UBC evaluation criteria

A safer five-step screen

  1. Start from the official transcript, not a portal summary.
  2. Label each school's formula before calculating anything.
  3. Apply course-load, year-level, pass/fail, and timing conditions before selecting years.
  4. Keep residency and pathway rules separate from the academic calculation.
  5. Re-open the official source before paying the application fee; retrieval dates are not guarantees of freshness.
Scope note: This guide explains why formulas differ. It does not calculate an official OMSAS GPA, determine eligibility, or predict admission. Program pages and application services always control. Requirements can change after publication.
